# National Insurance (Bituach Leumi) and Health Tax — 2026

## Summary

Israeli residents pay **National Insurance** (`ביטוח לאומי`) and **Health Tax** (`מס בריאות`), administered by Bituach Leumi (BTL). For the ordinary resident employee category below retirement age, the 2026 monthly ceiling is **₪51,910**, reduced-band threshold **₪7,703**, and combined employee rates **4.27% / 12.17%**. These are not universal rates for every residency, age or employment category. Ordinary investment capital gains are generally outside NI/health, but it is false that only labour income can be liable: some non-work income has its own rules.

## Rates / brackets / amounts (2026)

### Employee rates — salaried resident (`עובד שכיר`)

| Component | Low band (up to ₪7,703/mo) | High band (₪7,703–₪51,910/mo) |
|---|---|---|
| National Insurance — employee portion | **1.04%** | **7.00%** |
| Health Tax — employee portion | **3.23%** | **5.17%** |
| **Combined employee withholding** | **4.27%** | **12.17%** |

- Income above the ceiling of **₪51,910/month** (annualized ₪622,920) is **not** subject to NI or health.
- Use the current 2026 BTL table; do not infer the prior year's ceiling from a page whose amounts have since been updated.
- Source: cited BTL employee table; secondary guides do not override the primary rates.

### Self-employed rates (`עצמאי`)

| Component | Low band (up to ₪7,703/mo) | High band (₪7,703–₪51,910/mo) |
|---|---|---|
| National Insurance — self-employed | **4.47%** | **12.83%** |
| Health Tax — self-employed | **3.23%** | **5.17%** |
| **Combined** | **7.70%** | **18.00%** |

- Self-employed individuals pay both the employee-equivalent and (roughly) the employer-equivalent contributions because there is no separate employer.
- Eligible self-employed NI payments have a 52% income-tax deduction under §47A, subject to its conditions; this excludes health-insurance contributions. The cited BTL calculation guidance distinguishes NI from health and explains the NI-base adjustment.
- Source: current BTL self-employed table, age 18 to statutory retirement age. Other age/status categories have different rates and minimum assessment rules.

### "Not working" / passive-income earners

- For residents who are not working and have non-exempt non-work income, the cited BTL table has its own exemption band, minimum payment and 12.09% / 12.17% bands. Do not apply those percentages to all passive income or ignore statutory exclusions. The table's 2026 example, not a secondary headline rate, is the reference.

### Non-residents

- Non-resident employees of an Israeli employer: **0.1% low / 0.87% high** (NI only; no health tax for non-residents). Same ₪51,910 ceiling.

## Application notes

### Capital gains are **not** subject to NI/health

- Ordinary investment capital gains are generally outside NI/health. Dividend and interest exclusions depend on the applicable statutory income classification; do not extend that conclusion to every payment merely labelled passive income or to business income. PwC's capital-gains statement alone does not prove every dividend/interest exemption.
- This applies to the **capital slice** of an NVDA tranche sale, not automatically
  to its ordinary employment-income slice; see the payroll qualification below.
- Primary scope: §350(a)(6) excludes income taxed under Income Tax Ordinance
  §125B except §373A income, and interest taxed under §125C(b)/(c). Do not extend
  these exclusions to business-classified income or every interest payment.

### RSU vesting interaction (this is where it matters)

- The ordinary-income component of an equity realization can be subject to NI/health under applicable payroll rules. The settled household Section 102 tax event is **sale, not vest**; use `section_102.md` and actual payroll allocation, not a generic vest-time deduction.
- If actual insured payroll has already reached the monthly ceiling, additional ordinary income allocated to that same month has no further NI/health charge. Seniority or annual salary does not prove this; additional-pay allocation and the actual Section 102/payroll event must be checked. This note does not change the user's settled trustee-tax assumptions.
- Do not apply a flat "12% take-home cut" to every additional shekel: marginal
  NI/health depends on actual insured payroll and the applicable allocation period.
- Where that payroll already exhausts the monthly ceiling, additional income
  allocated to the same month adds no NI/health. This is a conditional calculation,
  not a current household-payroll fact inferred from job title or annual salary.

### Sell-to-cover and Section 102

- The household's settled trustee/Form 106 evidence shows no withholding at vest and both tax slices withheld at sale. Do not invent a sell-to-cover event from a portal setting. This file supplies NI categories/ceilings only, not a replacement for the Section 102 ledger.

## Stack with related rates

For a senior NVIDIA Israel employee at base salary > ₪51,910/month:

| Income source | NI/health marginal | Income tax marginal | Combined marginal at top |
|---|---|---|---|
| Base salary, below ₪7,703/mo (only the first slice) | 4.27% | per bracket | varies |
| Base salary, ₪7,703–₪51,910/mo | 12.17% | per bracket | varies |
| Base salary, > ₪51,910/mo | **0%** | up to 50% | **~50%** |
| RSU ordinary slice (above ceiling) | **0%** | 47% + 3% surtax | **~50%** |
| RSU capital slice (102 Capital) | **0%** | 25% + 3%/2% surtax | **~30%** |
| NVDA capital gain post-sale | **0%** | 25% + 3%/2% surtax | **~30%** |

## Refresh cadence

- **Annual (January)** — BTL re-indexes the ceiling and the reduced-band threshold; rates can also change by amendment.
- **Mid-year** — only on legislation; rare.
