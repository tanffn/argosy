Yes: the owner was substantially right, and my earlier $611,600 was misleading if presented as a permanent household saving. It was a first-death liquidity figure based on the incorrect assumption that roughly $3.058 million could be divided equally.

The additional benefit is not merely another $60,000 exemption: technically it is another $13,000 credit plus another run through the graduated brackets. The statutory rate above $1 million is $345,800 plus 40% of the excess, and the ordinary non-treaty NRNC credit is $13,000. [U.S. rate schedule](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=%28title%3A26+section%3A2001+edition%3Aprelim%29), [IRS Form 706-NA instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i706na).

There are two important qualifications:

1. **If Leumi is already recognized as beneficially 50/50 owned, separating the accounts may save essentially zero.** For non-U.S.-citizen spouses, the IRS generally starts with full inclusion of jointly held property and then permits exclusion based on contribution/beneficial-ownership evidence. Title alone does not decide it. [IRS joint-property treatment](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i706na).

2. **If first-death assets pass to the survivor, “saving collapses toward zero” is directionally useful but not a complete calculation.** There is normally no marital deduction for a noncitizen survivor absent a QDOT, the inherited assets may enter the survivor’s later estate, and a prior-transfer tax credit may apply if the deaths are sufficiently close. Funding the first tax from portfolio versus outside assets also changes the result. Consequently, no universal both-deaths number should be quoted without the wills, beneficiaries, contribution evidence and expected QDOT treatment.

The correct presentation is therefore:

- **Primary economic measure:** after-tax family wealth through both deaths, consistent with the actual dispositive plan.
- **Separate stress test:** first-death liquidity. On the corrected facts, a genuine $371.8k transfer reduces Ariel-first tax by about **$148.7k**, not $611.6k. That still matters because Form 706-NA and payment are generally due within nine months, and transfer-certificate/broker administration can constrain access to assets. [IRS filing and payment deadlines](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i706na).
