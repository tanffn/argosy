A better route than selling SCHD

Gift shares to Noga — into her sole accountneeds one fact

Your instinct not to sell was right. There's a route that gets much of the estate benefit at no income-tax cost at all:

  • US side: a non-resident's gift of US intangibles — company stock, ETF shares — is outside US gift tax entirely, at any size. No Form 709-NA.
  • Israeli side: a genuine gift between resident relatives is exempt under §97(a)(5). Deferred, not free — Noga inherits your basis and acquisition date. There's a Tax Authority "green route" for in-kind transfers through an exchange member.

Splitting SCHD saves roughly USD 32.8k of estate tax; moving all of it, roughly USD 43.5k.

The trap, and it's a big one: do not add Noga as a joint owner. For a non-US-citizen survivor there's no automatic 50/50 rule — §2040(a) pulls the entire joint asset into the first estate unless the survivor proves independent consideration, and assets you gifted her don't count as proof. It has to be a completed, irrevocable transfer into an account she solely owns and controls.

Honest caveat: this moves exposure rather than eliminating it, and it carries real divorce, creditor and succession consequences. Selling is the only thing that removes it permanently.

Possibly better still — harvest against lossesif you have them

If you have Israeli capital-loss carryforwards big enough to absorb the ~USD 67.9k gain, you sell the matched lots and buy the Irish fund: the US-situs asset is gone permanently, at little or no Israeli tax. That beats both gifting and holding. It depends entirely on what your loss schedule actually shows — question 3 below.
